## 05 A card = 5 value multipliers

A single card is the product of five independent multipliers. That is why two "identical" cards can differ thousandfold in $/Card.

*The same card in Golden — $3.0K/Card (×1.5). Venomous is $6.0K (×3.0).*

That is a clean measurement of the **finish multiplier** on one card: Normal $2.0K → Golden $3.0K → Venomous $6.0K. On top of finish sits **level** — and it dominates:

| Multiplier | What it does | Example / scale |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Name × Rarity | Base $/Card | Common $10 · Uncommon $65 · Rare $140 · … · Transcendent $320K |

| Finish | Multiplies base | Normal ×1 · Golden ×1.5 · Venomous ×3 · … · Diamond ×2 |

| Level (≤50) | Multiplies up to ×3300 | Fog Slayer $320K(L1) → $1.42M(L10) → $1.07B(L50 MAX) |

| Rank (F–LR) | Cash multiplier | F 1x → D 1.2x → A 2x → UR 10x (rolled on the Rank Machine) |

| Trait (Fortune) | +% Cash | Fortune I +10% · II +30% (rolled on the Traits Machine) |

### Formulas: leveling, upgrade cost, earnings

This is the core of the economy, reconstructed from the per-level price captures. Three formulas are enough to rebuild card balance from scratch:

• **Card value by level:** value(L) = base × 1.18^(L−1) — each level is **+18%** on $/Card. Measured on 5 cards (r = 1.179–1.182). **L1 → L50 = 1.18⁴⁹ = ×3328** — this is the "×3300 law". 
• **Level-up cost:** cost(L→L+1) = 0.25 × base × 1.35^(L−1) — the first upgrade (L1→2) always costs **25% of the card's base $/Card** (confirmed on 6 cards), then each level is **×1.35** (q = 1.34–1.35). Cost grows *faster* than value (1.35 > 1.18): later levels give less ROI per step, but the L50 total (×3328) pays it all back. 
• **Earnings:** $/s ≈ sum(board $/Card) × 0.15 (≈ ÷6.5). Pinned by placing cards one-by-one: a 3-card board ($140 + $65 + $11 = $216) makes exactly **$33/s** (0.153); same at endgame (Vision Striker $765.5T → $117.8T/s). The Cash multiplier applies at **sell** (box → cash), not to the /s accrual.

| Lvl | Thunder Assassin $/Card | Upgrade L→L+1 | × vs prev |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| L1 | $600 | $150 | — |

| L3 | $834 | $273 | ×1.35 |

| L5 | $1.16K | $498 | ×1.35 |

| L7 | $1.62K | $908 | ×1.35 |

| L10 | $2.66K | $2.23K | ×1.35 |

| L50 (MAX) | ≈ base×3328 | — | — |

**Building one card:** income = base(name×rarity) × finish × **1.18^(L−1)** × rank (up to 10x) × trait (up to 2x Cash), and passive $/s ≈ that value ÷ 5. Level is the dominant lever (×3328 at cap); rank and trait stack on top; the Cash multiplier finishes the payout at sell. One L50 chase card outweighs the whole early board — income jumped from $437.5K/s to **$117.8T/s** the moment a single L50 Vision Striker ($765.5T/Card) hit the plot.

The only non-gacha power lever: sacrifice several specific cards to get one far stronger. This is why a player keeps their "spare" cards.

*Card Craft — a grid of 13 evolution recipes: Flame Pillar $2.4M · Emperor King $70M · Silent Esper $6.5B · Genius Striker $890B · … · Black Reaper $2.0O/Card (top recipe).*

![Recipe detail: needs Wind Slayer + Serpent Slayer + Fog Slayer (all Transcendent, 1 each) + $1.8M and a Mutations slot → CRAFT.](images/024-recipe-detail-needs-wind-slayer-serpent-slayer-f.jpg)

*Recipe detail: needs Wind Slayer + Serpent Slayer + Fog Slayer (all Transcendent, 1 each) + $1.8M and a Mutations slot → CRAFT.*

Boss-specific shards (White Titan Shards — "used to craft the White Titan") are recipe inputs too. This is the main way to push the board up without gacha.

## 10 Economy & prices

Cash is the soft currency from selling cards. The whole curve is exponential and effectively uncapped — which is exactly what creates demand for Robux accelerators.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Packs | Buy the next rarity tier | $100 → $1K → $1M → … → $100B |

| Card leveling | Level to 50 (×3300 on value) | Transcendent L1→2 = $80K |

Selling returns ~**50%** of value (Void Pack $2.0Qd → $1.0Qd), and that is the main Cash source at endgame — you sell rare packs/cards whole. Cash Boost (+200% max) and Time Boost (−24% max) **cap**, but Base Expansion and Luck Boost do not: they are what eats the exponential net worth.

**Honesty caveat.** The capture's leap to $Qn/$Sx is **not a solo pace**: a VIP player, D3adpo0139, repeatedly gifted the recorder expensive packs ("go sell it", "upgrade the conveyor"). The systems (pack tiers, sell prices, the conveyor sink) are real and frame-confirmed, but **that speed of wealth is not solo-reproducible**. The recorder even typed in chat: "I'll play myself, I'm missing a lot."

## 18 What to take

The transferable design moves — the reason we tear other games apart.

1

#### A cozy mask on whale math

The "card factory" and ASMR open lower the barrier to entry, but underneath is a 5-multiplier gacha machine. Soft wrapper + hard math = a wide funnel and a deep spending ceiling.

2

#### Five independent multipliers on one object

A card = name×rarity × finish × level × rank × trait. Each multiplier is its own system and its own monetization point; together they yield a thousandfold spread from a single entity.

3

#### Level as the dominant lever

Leveling to MAX 50 = ×3300 on value — reliably, across every card. One clear-but-huge lever keeps a player in the "pull → level → re-place" loop longer than the gacha itself does.

4

#### A time wall sold in pieces

Pack timers grow with rarity (5 min → 1h+), and the base holds 8+ queues. Hours of waiting convert into Skip 5/10/30/120min and Skip All — time is sold in graded doses.

5

#### Layered return loops

Daily (24h) + PlayTime (session, resets on leave) + Free Rewards (ads, 4/day) + a social like goal — four different reasons to come back that do not step on each other.

6

#### Honesty in the teardown

The capture's endgame wealth was partly gifted by a VIP player. We describe the systems as real but never sell the gifted speed as a solo pace — otherwise the reconstruction lies about balance.
